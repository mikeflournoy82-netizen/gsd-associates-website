Fractional VP of Sales vs. Full-Time: Which Is Right for Your Stage?

M
Michael Flournoy
Fractional VP of Sales · June 2026 · 9 min read

This is the question I get asked most often by founders who've hit $1-2M ARR and know they need sales leadership: "Should I hire a fractional VP of Sales or a full-time one?"

I'll give you the honest answer, including the cases where fractional is the wrong choice.

What a Fractional VP of Sales Actually Does

First, let's dispense with the vague definition. A fractional VP of Sales isn't a part-time sales manager. They're a senior operator — someone who's built and scaled sales teams before — working 1-3 days per week at your company instead of full-time.

They own strategy (what's the sales motion?), leadership (are we managing reps correctly?), process (do we have a repeatable playbook?), and execution (is quota being hit?). They do not typically carry a bag themselves — they build the system that closes deals.

The fractional model exists because most companies at $1-5M ARR can't afford — and don't need — a full-time VP of Sales at $200K+ base salary.

When Fractional Wins

You're at $500K-$3M ARR and still figuring out your sales motion. At this stage, you need someone to build the playbook, not run it. A fractional VP can define ICP, build the pipeline process, set up the CRM correctly, hire and ramp your first 1-2 reps, and install a coaching rhythm — then step back once it's running.

You have 1-3 reps but no sales leadership. You have people selling, but no one coaching. No one reviewing calls. No one building career development plans. A fractional VP fills that gap at a fraction of the cost of a full-time hire.

You're pre-revenue or early-revenue and need to validate the sales model. Before you hire full-time, validate that you can actually sell the product. A fractional VP with relevant domain experience can compress that timeline from 12 months to 90 days.

You want the Whip Around model. When I was part of the founding team that built Whip Around's US sales operation from zero, we used outside expertise in the early days to establish the playbook before we had the budget to hire senior full-time talent. It worked. The acquisition at $100M is the outcome.

When Full-Time Is Right

You're past $5M ARR with a proven sales motion. At this stage, you need someone in the building. You have enough reps, enough deal flow, and enough complexity that a 1-2 day per week leader can't keep up. You need someone whose full attention is on your business.

You're raising a Series A or B. Investors want to see full-time leadership in place. A fractional VP is a transitional solution — it signals you're not ready to commit to a permanent leader. For a fundraise, you want a full-time VP who can speak to the board as a peer.

Your reps need daily management. If you have 5+ reps with different deal cycles, territories, and performance levels, they need a full-time manager who knows their deals, their struggles, and their clients. A fractional VP flying in once a week can't provide that depth.

You've found the right person for the role. If the right hire appears — someone with direct experience in your vertical at your stage, who wants the full-time role — take them. The perfect fractional-to-full-time transition is finding a fractional VP who becomes the full-time hire after proving themselves in the role.

The Cost Math

Full-time VP of Sales (experienced):

  • Base salary: $180K-$250K
  • OTE: $300K-$400K
  • Equity: 0.5%-1.5%
  • Benefits, overhead: $40K-$60K/year
  • Total cost: $350K-$450K/year before hitting quota

Fractional VP of Sales (experienced, 2 days/week):

  • Monthly retainer: $6K-$15K depending on experience and time commitment
  • Annual cost: $72K-$180K
  • No equity (typically)
  • No benefits overhead
  • No severance risk

The math isn't close. For a company at $1-3M ARR, a fractional VP delivers 80% of the impact at 25-35% of the cost. The remaining 20% — the full-time presence, the daily availability — only matters after you've crossed the threshold where you need it.

Signs You Need Each

Signs you need a fractional VP:

  • Close rate is below 20% and you don't know why
  • Reps are doing activity but not booking qualified meetings
  • You haven't documented your sales process anywhere
  • You're handling sales escalations yourself as the founder
  • You want to hire your first AE but don't know how to manage one

Signs you need a full-time VP:

  • You have 5+ reps and no one is getting consistent coaching
  • You're going into a fundraising round
  • Your sales cycle is 6+ months and deals require constant relationship management
  • You have budget to compete for senior talent
