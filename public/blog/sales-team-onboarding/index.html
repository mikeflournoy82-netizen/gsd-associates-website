The 90-Day Sales Onboarding Plan That Gets New Reps to Quota

M
Michael Flournoy
Fractional VP of Sales · June 2026 · 11 min read

Most companies treat sales onboarding like orientation day: hand the new rep a login, walk them through the deck, schedule a few ride-alongs, and tell them quota starts in 30 days.

Then at day 90, they wonder why the rep is 40% to quota and struggling.

I've built and rebuilt sales onboarding programs at multiple companies, including during the Whip Around growth run that ended in a $100M acquisition. The pattern of what works — and what doesn't — is consistent.

What Most Companies Get Wrong

The single biggest mistake in sales onboarding is confusing product knowledge with sales readiness.

New reps spend weeks learning about features, architecture, integrations, and competitive positioning. Then they get in front of a prospect, and they lead with features instead of problems. They can't run discovery because they were never trained to run discovery. They know everything about what the product does and nothing about how a buyer actually makes a purchasing decision.

Product knowledge is a supporting tool. Sales skills are the job.

The 30/60/90 Framework

Days 1-30: Foundation

The first 30 days are about building the knowledge foundation and getting a rep to their first real sales conversation. Not quota. Not pipeline. Just a real conversation with a real prospect where they can hold their own.

Week 1-2:

  • Customer deep-dive — read every case study, listen to 10 recorded customer calls, talk to 3 actual customers
  • Competitive landscape — not from your deck, from the competitor's own materials and G2/Capterra reviews
  • ICP definition — who exactly are we selling to and why do they buy?

Week 3-4:

  • Discovery framework — how do we run a first call? What questions do we ask? What are we listening for?
  • Demo certification — rep must complete a full demo in front of manager before taking a live demo call
  • First 10 outbound prospecting attempts (shadowed, reviewed, debriefed)

Milestone: By day 30, the rep has completed a demo cert and booked at least 2 discovery calls on their own.

Days 31-60: Pipeline Building

Month two is about building real pipeline and going through the full sales motion start-to-finish. The rep isn't expected to close deals yet, but they should be running complete cycles.

  • Outbound quota kicks in (lighter than full quota — 50-60%)
  • Weekly call review with manager — one recorded call per week, reviewed together
  • First solo demos — manager shadows for the first 3, then reps fly solo
  • Objection handling drills — the 5-7 objections you hear on every call, rehearsed until the rep can handle them cold

Milestone: By day 60, the rep has $X in qualified pipeline (set this number based on your ACV and expected close rate).

Days 61-90: Full Ramp

Month three is the proving ground. Full activity quota. Full pipeline expectations. Manager shifts from coaching to reviewing.

  • Full outbound quota
  • Weekly forecast call — rep owns their own forecast, presents it with confidence
  • First close attempts — deals that have been in pipeline since week 3-4 should be reaching decision stage
  • Independent deal strategy — rep brings their own deal plans, not just asking "what should I do?"

Milestone: By day 90, the rep has closed at least one deal or has multiple deals deep in the pipeline with clear close dates.

The Things Nobody Tells You

Ramp time is longer than you think. The industry benchmark is 3-6 months for AE full productivity. If you're expecting full quota in 30 days, you're setting your rep up to fail and yourself up for expensive turnover.

The manager is half the equation. A great rep with a bad manager underperforms. A good rep with a great manager over-delivers. If your managers aren't doing weekly 1:1s with structured call reviews, your onboarding program doesn't matter.

Document the "why," not just the "what." New reps can memorize your pitch deck. What they can't memorize is why a customer bought, what changed in their business, what alternative they considered. The "why" comes from customer conversations. Force those conversations early.

One Metric to Track

Time-to-first-close. Track it for every rep you hire. If it's consistently longer than 90 days, your onboarding program has a gap. If some reps close in 45 days and others take 120, look at the difference in how they were onboarded and who their manager was.

This single metric will tell you more about onboarding effectiveness than any satisfaction survey.
